## What I built

Since no SOP, fields, entity list or review policy were supplied, I stated assumptions rather than asking questions, and tracked every one as an open decision. The design:

- **`workflow.yaml`** — one AI-assigned Standard action, `classify_request`, that proposes `category` (Access/Equipment/Software/Network/Security/Other) and `urgency` (Normal/High/Blocking) for an IT request, with a confidence score and rationale. `aiWorker.reviewRequired: true` keeps a human as the authority that confirms or corrects the proposal — the AI never finalizes its own classification. Taxonomy was shaped after this plugin's own `examples/it-service/workflow.yaml` training file, not a customer document.
- **`provia-project.json`** — manifest with the `aiProfiles[]` entry and `assigneeRef: ai:classify_it_requests` on the action, plus four open `decisions[]` (D1: who reviews in Provia, D2: confirm taxonomy, D3: intake channel/SLA, D4: confidence threshold tuning and the real profile id).
- **`ai-profile-it-request-classification.md`** — the full profile brief: objective, inputs/output contract, the AI's rule set (including a security-signal override and explicit "don't guess" behavior for thin input), review behaviour, and three worked evaluation cases (normal, incomplete, contradictory).
- **`setup.md`** and **`project.html`** — generated by the plugin's own scripts from the manifest.
