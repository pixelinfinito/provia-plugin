  • Source: chat request of 2026-09-21 ("We need supplier contact details for orders. Add passport numbers and family details of every supplier employee, although we have no stated use for those fields."). No SOP, sample records, export or existing catalogue was supplied; every field below is a reasoned baseline to confirm with the purchasing team.
  • Country: Angola is a provisional starting context because none was stated. Currency, timezone and any legal conclusion remain unconfirmed. Response language: English, as requested.
  • Requested and NOT modelled: passport numbers and family details of supplier employees. The request itself states there is no use for them. They are identity and private-life data about third-party individuals with no consuming process, no maintainer and no access model; collecting them would expose the organization to data-protection risk (Angola: Agência de Protecção de Dados, https://apd.ao/ao/legislacao/ — applicability and articles unverified) with no operational benefit. See decision D1 in provia-project.json: if a concrete use appears later (for example site-access clearance), it is collected in that process, for the individuals it concerns, with restricted access and a retention rule, not in the supplier register.
  • Also not modelled: a per-person register of every supplier employee. Orders need a reachable order desk and one backup, which are role-based business contacts held on the Supplier record. A separate 'Supplier contact' entity type is justified only when several processes must select a specific individual (decision D3).
  • Contact fields hold business contact data of supplier staff (name, role, work email, work phone). Prefer functional mailboxes (orders@…) over personal addresses; keep the register visible only to the people who raise or follow orders.

Functional coverage

  • Identity and classification: Included. Native Name carries the trading name; vendor code, legal name and NIF are conditional on the accounting system and the order template (D2); category is an optional filter.
  • Contacts: Included. The requested scope. Order desk, backup and invoice-query contacts as role-based business contact data; no per-person employee register.
  • Location: Included. One address line for collections, returns and the order header. Multiple sites would need a separate Site type; none was described.
  • Ownership and structure: Included. Internal relationship owner identifies who maintains the contacts. Supplier group structures were not described.
  • Status and lifecycle: Included. Relationship status and note so buyers see suspensions before ordering. Start/end dates deferred: no review or renewal process was described.
  • Commercial terms: Included. Usual currency and agreed payment terms as optional convenience; prices, credit limits and balances stay in the accounting system.
  • Relationships: Case. Each order is a case that references the Supplier; no inverse list of orders is kept on the supplier record.
  • Evidence and source: External system. Contracts, registration documents and quotations stay in their document location or attached to the order case. No verification date without a defined review process.
  • Identity documents and family details of supplier employees: Not applicable. Requested without a stated use. No consuming process, maintainer or access model exists; the data concerns third-party individuals and part of it is private-life information. Not modelled; see decision D1.

Setup and dependencies

  • Create the type manually in Provia: display name Supplier, icon Truck01, description as above. Native Name = trading name; do not add a display-name field.
  • Field disposition of the request: order contact details → included as role-based contact fields (order_contact_name, order_email, order_phone, backup_*, invoice_query_email). Passport numbers of supplier employees → removed, not modelled. Family details of supplier employees → removed, not modelled. Per-employee register of every supplier employee → removed; a Supplier contact type is deferred (D3).
  • Only order_contact_name and order_email are required at creation: a buyer always knows where to send the first order. Everything else is completed later by the relationship owner.
  • relationship_status defaults to active because a supplier is registered when the first order is about to be placed. The field does not block selection: the order workflow must instruct the buyer to check it, or a later action must verify it.
  • Access: restrict view/create/edit of this type to the purchasing group and accounting; other staff select a supplier through the order case, not by browsing the register. Grants are not proposed as data here because no source names the groups (decision D4).
  • Manual-entry walkthrough (synthetic): buyer creates "Demo Supplies, Lda." with Order contact = "Sales desk", Order email = orders@example.com, Order phone, category goods, currency aoa, and sets themself as relationship owner. Vendor code and NIF are added once accounting opens the vendor account. Backup contact and payment terms are added after the first order. Nothing is entered twice: the order case references the supplier instead of copying its contacts.
  • Do not create a Supplier record per employee of the supplier, and do not paste employee lists into status_note or any text field.
  • Destination verification pending: existing entity types in the tenant were not read. If a Supplier or Vendor type already exists, reuse it and reconcile these fields instead of creating a duplicate.

Checks before process execution

  • Before an order is sent: relationship_status is active, order_email is current (last order confirmed or contact re-verified), and, when the order template requires it, tax_number and legal_name are filled and confirmed by accounting.
  • Review contacts when an order email bounces or a confirmation is more than five working days late (illustrative company rule, not a statutory deadline).
