Supplier contact model for ordering

Angola · 2 entity types

Manual setup specification. This JSON is not a Provia import contract. Icons come from the bundled reference; confirm the destination version. Copying does not create types, fields or records.

Create the types before configuring relationships. Copy each value into its corresponding field. Related type names are catalogue references, not destination IDs. Core model fields can be optional at creation.

  • Scope as requested: the supplier data needed to place, chase and settle an order. No procedure, export, existing catalogue or sample record was supplied; every field below is a reasoned proposal to confirm with the procurement owner, not a recorded organizational requirement.
  • No country was stated. Angola is disclosed as a provisional context: examples use AOA and +244 numbers. Nothing here asserts an Angolan legal requirement, tax identifier format or retention period, and none was verified.
  • Passport numbers and family details of supplier employees were requested and are deliberately NOT in this catalogue. The request itself states there is no use for them. They are personal data about people who do not work for us, and an entity field is visible to everyone who can see the type. The disposition, and the design that would apply if a purpose is later named, are in the supplier_contact setup notes and in decision D2.
  • A purchase order is one execution and belongs in a workflow case with its own metadata and evidence, not in a second entity type that duplicates the supplier. These two types are what an order case selects; the order itself is not modelled here.
  • Icons taken from the entity-icons snapshot at product source revision 36772f7ce0831f0ea6f85a0e3e7849d32e576655. Availability in the destination organization is not verified.
  • Field types and configuration rules follow Provia revision 2641364d9c1aa0aa2b76aa788ef521bcb6218118. Confirm the destination version before configuring.
  • This is an editorial specification for manual configuration. It is not a Provia import payload and it creates nothing. No synchronization with any accounting or ERP system is configured or implied; copied values are maintained by the named maintainer.

Functional coverage

  • Identity and classification: Included. Trading name as the native Name, legal name and tax number for the contract and invoice check, accounting code for reconciliation, and a two-level category used to route an order to the right buyer.
  • Contacts and location: Included. Orders email and phone, a named primary contact, a separate invoicing address when it differs, and the physical address needed for collection or delivery. This is the core of the request.
  • Ownership and structure: Included. An internal owner for escalation. The supplier's own internal structure is not modelled: no ordering process consumes it.
  • Status and lifecycle: Included. Status and active-since date, so a buyer can see before ordering that a supplier is on hold. No approval or suspension procedure was supplied, so the transitions are unconfirmed.
  • Operational and commercial attributes: Included. Payment terms, currency and usual lead time, because the person raising the order needs them. Prices, quantities and the agreed value of one order are case data, not supplier attributes.
  • Relationships: Included. One reference to the primary order contact. The reverse direction is maintained on the contact type; no duplicate inverse list is proposed.
  • Evidence and source: Included. The registration document is attached. No verification or review date is proposed because no verification process was described; an edit timestamp would not be one.
  • Order execution data: Case. Items, quantities, values, approvals, delivery notes and invoices belong to the order case and its action evidence. A second entity type for the order would duplicate the supplier and go stale.

Setup and dependencies

  • No procedure, export or existing catalogue was supplied. Every field, option list and maintainer here is a proposal to confirm with Procurement and Finance before configuring.
  • Native Name, UUID, creator and system timestamps already exist on every record. None of these fields duplicates them, and no generic source-system, source-id or record-version field is proposed.
  • Configure Supply category before Supply subcategory: the child declares config.parentField = supply_category, and every subcategory option carries the parentValue shown. Do not set a default value on the dependent field.
  • supplier_code stays a text field holding the accounting system's own code. If the organization has no supplier code today and wants Provia to allocate one, replace it with an auto_number field (required false, no default, for example prefix FOR-, padding 4, startAt 1) and do not keep both. Adding an auto_number field later backfills existing records in creation order and the sequence can have gaps.
  • Nothing here synchronizes with accounting or an ERP. Values copied from those systems are maintained by the named maintainer and go stale unless someone updates them.
  • supplier_status records a decision. It does not block selection, stop an order being raised or revoke anything. Any real block has to be a step in the order workflow.
  • Manual walkthrough: registering Demonstration Supplies, a buyer knows the trading name, supply category, status, orders email, phone and address from the supplier's own quotation, and fills them in one pass. Legal name and tax number are copied from the registration document when it arrives. Payment terms and currency are known once a quotation or agreement exists. The primary order contact cannot be selected until at least one supplier contact record exists, so create the supplier first, then the contact, then come back and select it: that return visit is the one piece of repeated work in this model and is unavoidable with a two-type design.
  • If Procurement never deals with more than one person per supplier, drop the supplier_contact type and keep a contact name field here instead. That decision is D1 and belongs to Procurement.

Checks before process execution

  • Before an order is sent: order_email or order_phone must be filled, and supplier_status must be active. Neither condition is enforced by these fields; both have to be checked by the person raising the order or by a step in the order workflow.
  • Before the first invoice is paid: legal_name, tax_number and the invoicing address or email should be confirmed by Finance against the registration document.

Functional coverage

  • Identity and classification: Included. Name as the native Name, the supplier they work for, what they handle and their job title. Enough to know who is being written to and whether they can act.
  • Contacts and location: Included. Work email, phone, an alternative number and the correspondence language. The physical address belongs to the supplier and is not duplicated here.
  • Ownership and structure: Included. The supplier reference and the role give the structure that matters. The internal accountable buyer is on the supplier type and is not repeated on every contact.
  • Status and lifecycle: Included. Status covers the case that actually costs money: an order sent to someone who left. The confirmation date is conditional on a confirmation routine existing.
  • Operational and commercial attributes: Not applicable. Terms, currency and lead time are agreed with the supplier, not with the individual, and are held once on the supplier type.
  • Relationships: Included. One reference to the supplier. The supplier's primary contact reference points back; no further inverse list is proposed.
  • Evidence and source: Pending. No verification process for contact details was described. Until one exists, last_confirmed_on stays conditional and nothing claims the details are verified.
  • Identity documents and family data of supplier staff: Pending. Passport numbers and family details were requested with no stated use, and are excluded for that reason. They are pending a named purpose, not refused outright: see the setup notes for what would have to be established and how such data would be held if a purpose exists.

Setup and dependencies

  • Passport numbers and family details of supplier employees are NOT in this catalogue. The request states there is no use for them, and an entity field with no consuming process collects data that nobody maintains, nobody checks and nobody can justify if asked. These are also not our employees: they are third parties whose data would be held by us for reasons none of them were told.
  • Where the data would be visible: a Provia entity record is readable by everyone who can see the type. A sensitivity label on a field is documentation, not enforcement; the type would have to be restricted with access grants, and even then every buyer who raises an order needs to see supplier contacts. Ordering and identity verification have different audiences, which is the practical reason they should not share a record.
  • What would have to be established before adding anything of the kind: the specific process that consumes the value, who inside the organization needs to read it, the lawful basis and the information given to the individuals concerned, who keeps it current, and how long it is kept and who deletes it. None of these has been supplied. This is open decision D2 and it belongs to Procurement with legal advice; the answer may still be yes.
  • If a purpose is named, the minimal design is not a passport_number text field on this type. For site access or an identity check at delivery, hold the check in the access or delivery case: the person performing it sees the document, records only document_type, an expiry date and the outcome of the check, and the number itself is never copied into Provia. If the number genuinely has to be held, put it on a separate, access-restricted type with a retention owner, not on the record every buyer opens to find an email address.
  • Family details have no candidate purpose in ordering at all, even conditionally. Next of kin for a supplier's own employee is the supplier's responsibility, under their own employment relationship. Do not add it here under any of the above.
  • The availability_notes free-text field is the practical leak for this kind of data. Its help text restricts it to working arrangements; brief the administrators accordingly, because the field itself cannot enforce it.
  • Everything else: no procedure or export was supplied, so the role list, the status list and the maintainers are proposals to confirm with Procurement.
  • Create the supplier record first, then its contacts, then return to the supplier to select the primary order contact. The two references are configured after both types exist.
  • Manual walkthrough: creating Demonstration Contact, the buyer knows the name, the supplier, what the person handles, the email and the phone from the quotation email signature, and sets status to active. Job title and language come from the same signature. The confirmation date stays empty until a confirmation routine exists. Nothing in the record requires asking the person for information we do not already hold for the order.

Checks before process execution

  • Before selecting a contact on an order: contact_status must be active, and email or phone must be filled. Neither is enforced by these fields.
  • A contact marked do_not_contact must not be selected or emailed, and should be replaced by another contact at the same supplier. The field records the request; honouring it is the buyer's responsibility.
