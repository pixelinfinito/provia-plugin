# Country context

Establish country of operation, applicable jurisdiction, response language, currency, timezone, sector and the organization’s own policy. Do not derive jurisdiction from the language of a document. If no country is supplied, disclose Angola as a provisional starting context. Ask before relying on a jurisdiction for a legal conclusion. An English conversation can still concern Angola; Portuguese can concern another country.

Use Angolan Portuguese for Portuguese outputs in the Angola context. Prefer utilizador, ficheiro, equipa, registo, ecrã, descarregar and protecção. Follow exact UI labels when guiding navigation, explaining a different label if necessary. Respect another language or terminology requested by the organization.

For Angolan examples, use AOA/Kz and Africa/Luanda. Keep YAML property names, enums, numeric values and required UTC timestamp formats unchanged. A displayed amount such as 250.000,00 Kz becomes a numeric 250000 in YAML. Separate display dates from machine-readable dates. Never assume Portuguese law, Brazilian LGPD/CLT, EUR or BRL applies merely because the text is Portuguese.

For cross-border work, identify each affected country and the parties/records to which a rule applies. Distinguish statutory requirements, company policy and a proposed best practice. If the source does not establish applicability, report an open question.

Legal claims require current primary sources, a relevant provision, jurisdiction, effective date and verification date. A source archive can contain repealed texts or proposals. Read the actual instrument, check its status and amendments, and preserve uncertainty. Do not invent tax rates, mandatory fields, statutory deadlines, retention periods or approval thresholds. If browsing is unavailable, use a supplied authoritative document with its date and mark current validity unverified. Do not block unrelated workflow design.

Do not place country context as invented top-level YAML properties. Keep it in the design brief or setup handover. Workday offsets in the pinned Provia implementation skip Saturday and Sunday; they do not implement national public holidays or a statutory deadline calculation.
